How the world is made

Back to Hillclimb

The map is a 160 × 92 grid on a cylinder: east and west wrap, north and south are the poles. Row r has latitude 90 − 180(r + 0.5)/92. Everything below is computed from the date alone, in this order. Nothing is placed by hand; the biomes are whatever the temperature and rainfall come to.

The figures are drawn from , using the game’s own code.

1. Height

Two fields of layered value noise, each a sum of octaves at halving amplitude:

shape = Σo=0..6 2−o · noiseo     crease = Σo=0..5 2−o · (1 − |2 noiseo − 1|)

shape gives rounded continents. crease is the same noise folded about its midpoint, which produces ridge lines rather than blobs; it is later multiplied by height above sea level, so it only bites where the ground is already high.

Each octave’s lattice is periodic east–west, so the seam is invisible, and carries a random vertical offset. Without that offset every octave would put a lattice row exactly on the north pole, the equator and the south pole of every planet. A row on a lattice row is read from it directly; a row between two is their average, and an average has a narrower spread. A sea level set by percentile turns that into a permanent surplus of land at those three latitudes. Offsetting each octave independently cancels it: measured over 1200 planets, the pole-to-equator difference in land falls from about five points to one.

2. The warp

Both fields are read at a displaced point rather than at the square itself:

h(r, c) = shape(r + k · dy,  c + k · dx)    where k = 40 (0.25 + 0.75 a)

dx and dy are two more noise fields in the range ±0.5, and a is a third, very broad one that varies how hard the displacement pulls from place to place.

The displacement fields use a tighter lattice than the terrain they displace. That is what makes the difference: a displacement varying more slowly than the terrain would only translate continents, while one varying faster folds the field over itself. Measured effect, holding land area fixed: small islands roughly double, and the landmarks that depend on them stop being rare — archipelagos appear on 49 days in 80 rather than 30, inland seas on 57 rather than 40, volcanic islands at all.

3. Sea level

Sea level is the 60th percentile of h, so land is 40% of every planet regardless of what the noise did. Height above it is scaled to metres by

metres = 7700 · (above · (0.42 + 1.5 · crease))1.55

with above the height above sea level normalised to 0–1. The exponent concentrates the high ground: most land is low, and summits are rare.

4. Ocean currents

Each sea square measures the distance to land to its east (e) and west (w) and takes a temperature offset

ΔT = 6.5 · exp(−((|lat| − 38)/24)2) · (e − w)/(e + w)

Sea against a western shore is on the poleward-flowing side of its basin and runs warm; sea against an eastern shore runs cold. The Gaussian confines the effect to the mid-latitudes, where the real gyres are. Nothing rotates; the coasts simply end up on the correct sides.

5. Temperature

T = 33 − 54 (|lat*|/90)1.8 − 6.3 · upland/1000 + ΔTcoast + noise

33 °C at the equator falling to −21 °C at the poles, minus a lapse rate of 6.3 °C per kilometre. upland is elevation averaged over a nine-square radius, not the square’s own height: climate responds to the height of a region, and using raw height made every crinkle in a warped coastline its own climate. Land within seven squares downwind of sea takes a share of that sea’s current offset, falling off with distance.

lat* is latitude displaced by up to ±9.5° by a broad noise field. Without it every climate band would change along the same row and the planet would come out in stripes.

6. Wind and rain

Wind direction is set by latitude: westward below 30° (trade winds), eastward from 30° to 60° (westerlies), westward above 60° (polar easterlies). A parcel of air with water content m traverses each row twice in that direction, so it arrives at the seam already loaded. Per square:

over sea:   m ← m + (1 − m)(0.10 + 0.010 T)

over land:   m ← 0.988 · m (1 − min(1, 0.07 + 0.42 Δz))

Δz is the climb in kilometres from the previous square. The 0.42 term is the whole of orography: rain falls on the windward slope, and the air that crosses the crest has less left. Recorded rainfall is m scaled by a zonal factor — wet where the trades converge on the equator, dry under the subtropical highs near 27°, wet again in the mid-latitude storm belt near 50°, dry over the poles.

7. Biomes

Rainfall is averaged over a three-square radius, then converted to a rank between 0 and 1 — separately for land above 6 °C and land below it. The split matters. Cold air carries little water, so cold ground is dry ground; ranked against the whole world, the polar half of the land occupies the bottom of the scale and the warm half is never dry enough to be desert. Ranked apart, dry means dry for that temperature, and the proportions come out close to Earth’s: deserts about 16% of land, permanent ice about 14%.

Each land square then takes the first rule that matches, with T in °C, z in metres and m the rainfall rank:

conditionbiome
T ≤ −12 and z ≥ 1600snowfield
T ≤ 0.5 and z ≥ 900bare mountain
T ≤ −13ice cap
T ≤ −2tundra
T ≤ 6m ≥ 0.42 boreal forest, else tundra
T ≤ 19m ≥ 0.62 forest, ≥ 0.42 grassland, ≥ 0.24 shrubland, else desert
otherwisem ≥ 0.70 rainforest, ≥ 0.52 seasonal forest, ≥ 0.30 savannah, else desert

Sea freezes below −12 °C; otherwise it is shallow within the top 12% of depth and open ocean below that.

8. Landmarks and the drop

Landmarks are read off the finished map. An archipelago is three or more landmasses of 18 squares or fewer within 10 columns and 8 rows of one another; an inland sea is a body of water not connected to the largest one; a volcanic island is a landmass of 70 squares or fewer reaching 1900 m. The rest are the biomes themselves. Each kind holds every instance of itself on the planet, so any desert is the desert.

The terrain comes from the date, so it is the same for everyone. The drop comes from a value stored in your browser, and the chain of landmarks is built around wherever it lands: each leg is costed by walking it, and the chain stops when the next leg would not fit in 28 moves. Drops are restricted to between 9 and 20 moves from the summit, so the one thing that differs between players is also the thing that differs least in difficulty.
